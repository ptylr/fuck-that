The highly questionable science

Weather quality runs from 100 (perfect golf weather) to 0 (borderline Jupiter). We score four ingredients: wind 35%, rain 35%, temperature 15%, feels-like 15%. Calm, dry, 18–24°C is the ideal. Rain and wind hurt more than needing a jumper.

Fuck That! = [0.8 × (100 − weather quality) + 0.2 × (100 − course quality)] ÷ 10. Weather does most of the work; a brilliant course buys some tolerance. At the extremes we anchor perfect weather + Cypress Point to 0, and disastrous weather + Bramcote Waters to 10. Those course endpoints are the group’s joke, not a published ranking.

When at least two forecasts predict severe conditions (sustained wind ≥30 mph, gusts ≥45 mph, thunder, snow/ice, or feels-like ≤−5°C / ≥38°C), the score is at least 9.5. A great course doesn’t make lightning a good idea. This is a planning joke, not a course-open status or official weather warning service.

Four forecast models. BBC in the mix too. UK Met Office, ECMWF IFS, NOAA GFS and DWD ICON forecasts are requested through Open-Meteo. Complete model forecasts get equal weight: their daytime measurements are averaged before scoring. Where BBC RSS provides a fresh maximum temperature, the temperature component blends the model average (75%) with BBC daily maximum (25%). BBC’s condition summary is shown as a cross-check, never converted into invented millimetres of rain. The feed currently has no wind, rainfall amount or feels-like data. Model disagreement is flagged at a spread of at least 5°C temperature, 10 mph wind or 4 mm daytime precipitation. That is not a calibrated confidence interval: model families can share inputs and some Open-Meteo variables can be derived. Incomplete models are excluded for that day; the breakdown names them. The 9.5 minimum needs at least two severe forecasts: each complete model counts once, and severe BBC condition wording counts once. Multiple severe hours or hazards in one forecast still count as one. A lone severe forecast stays visible but does not force the minimum, including when only one model is available. Days 4–7 use the available models without BBC.

Course quality is our editorial conversion, not a publisher’s score out of 100. Different ranking regions cannot be compared numerically. The calibration below uses ranking tiers where available and club descriptions otherwise; it stays fixed until edited.
